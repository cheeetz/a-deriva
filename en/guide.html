À Deriva

A fishing game that lives in a transparent strip at the bottom of the screen — or behind your desktop icons. It fishes on its own while you work; clicking pays more, but keeping watch was never required.

Five places in Brazil, from the Araguaia to Fernando de Noronha. Sixty fish to collect, a pet on every map, and the weather turning over on its own above all of it.

This page is what sits behind the numbers — if you just want to fish, you do not need it.

The cycle

Fish bite on their own. On average every 18 s, with a spread of 0.7× to 1.3× — and faster as your rod, boat and pets improve.

A “!” shows up in the strip. You have 2 seconds to click.

Clicked inside the window: perfect hook — the fish is worth +50% and cannot get away.

Did not click: the game rolls your bait's failure chance. On a failure the fish is gone; otherwise it goes into the boat as usual.

Boat full, fishing stops. Sell everything from the bar or buy a bigger box.

At night the good fish show up more: rare +20%, epic +40% e legendary +60% at the deepest hour of the night (the effect fades in and out with the transition). The Midnight Rod only works in that window — and the Blood Moon happens only there.

Rarity and bait

Bait is the most important decision in the game: it changes everything at once — the chance of a good fish and the chance of losing it. Better bait misses less.

Every map sells its own bait, with different names but the same five tiers. This is the chance that a fish which bit actually reaches the boat. Your rod and your pets still move it, in both directions.

Good bait pays for itself. Besides landing more, each tier carries a bonus of its own — speed on the uncommon, sale value on the top three. The exception is the legendary: it costs more than it returns. It is not bait for profit — it is the only one that makes the celestial appear, and that is how a collection gets finished.

The price climbs from map to map. The same legendary bait costs 10,000 in the Araguaia and 32,000 in Noronha — roughly triple, first to last. It is what stops endgame money from buying the early game all over again.

Weather events

Every 1 min the game rolls; the chance of an event starting is 5% (the first one only after 30 s of play) — on average, one event every 20 min. It warns you 10 s ahead and lasts 5 to 7 min, rolled on the spot. Every event has one guaranteed bite — somewhere between 20% and 80% of the way through, a fish of that rarity arrives, no roll needed.

Weight is how much the event weighs in the roll, not a percentage: Rain alone accounts for more than half of all rolls. Map events only enter the roll when you are there — and the Pororoca is the tidal wave coming up the river, so it applies to both rivers and nowhere else.

Storm, Pororoca and Hurricane charge for it. They fill the water with good fish but raise the failure rate — which is exactly when to switch to better bait and keep an eye on the “!”.

Boats and boxes

The boat sets how many fish fit before fishing stops. Every boat bought makes the next one 15% dearer. The last two require a trophy, not just money: it is what forces you to finish a collection before upgrading the fleet.

The boxes multiply the boat's capacity and stack — each one requires the previous: Styrofoam ×1.5 (25,000), Cooler ×2 (80,000), Hold ×2.5 (250,000 + 1 trophy), Cold Room ×3 (600,000 + 2 trophies).

The last two ask for a trophy for the same reason the Factory Ship and the Ocean Trawler do: capacity is what shortens play time the most, and the biggest one does not come from just waiting for coins to pile up. A trophy is a map's complete collection, and every collection goes through the celestial fish — which means through the expensive bait.

Pets

Every map has an animal. It shows up on its own, at 0.1% chance per fish caught. You will fish a long while before seeing your first. You can keep 3 active at once, and the bonuses stack.

Maps and trophies

You start on the Araguaia. Each new map opens by paying — or free, if you hold the previous one's trophy. The trophy comes from completing the collection of that place: all 12 fish, at least one of each.

The Celestial column is what changed the whole game: the rarest fish of each map gets progressively harder to land. On the Araguaia it comes at full odds; in Noronha, at a tenth of that. Because the trophy demands the complete collection, and the collection includes the celestial, this is what makes an endgame exist — and it is where the big money gets burned, on expensive bait.

The Collector

A boat that comes by once an hour, empties your hold and pays a share of the value. You only have one at a time, and buying a better one replaces the old. It is what makes the game actually earn on its own — at a toll.

CollectorCostsPaysWhen it is worth it

🛶 Dinghy40,00050% Right after the Factory Ship, when the hold starts filling without you watching
🚤 Ship120,00070% After the Ocean Trawler, when every cast is already worth a lot

None of them pays as well as selling by hand. You trade money for not having to be there — and sometimes that is worth it.

Where the game lives

It runs two ways, and the switch is in the menu (▲ button) under Settings. Switching mode restarts the game — on purpose: the two use different renderers, and that is chosen only at startup. The choice is remembered for next time.

Strip

A band above the taskbar, always in view. Clicks pass through where there is no game, so it does not get in the way of what is underneath.

Wallpaper

The whole landscape behind your desktop icons. The icons stay clickable and any window goes over the top. Windows only.

In wallpaper mode the HUD bar moves to the bottom right (the icons live on the left), and panels open in a little window of their own — close with Esc, by clicking outside, or through the fish icon in the system tray, next to the clock.

Also under Settings: with more than one monitor you can choose which screen the game lives on (switching also restarts), and there is a Start with Windows, which makes the game open by itself when the computer turns on.

The sound

The game has music, effects and weather — and all three turn on and off separately, each with its own volume, under Settings › Sound. They are separate because they bother people for different reasons: the music tires whoever leaves the game on all day, the effects get in the way of a call, and rain is exactly what a lot of people want in the background while they work.

Music

Thirteen tracks that take turns on their own and never repeat the same one twice in a row. Seven play by day and six by night — and the switch only happens at the end of a track, never cutting in the middle.

Effects

Only what happens on the river: the bite, the float, the fish in the basket, the perfect hook, the trophy, the pet. Buttons make no sound — a click is not news.

Weather

Rain, storm and wind underneath the music, for as long as the event lasts. In a storm and a hurricane there is lightning, and the thunder lands on the same frame as the flash — the crack runs half a second longer than the drawing, the way it does when it strikes close.

Three things that speed everything up

Click the “!”

+50% on the fish and zero failure. It is the only moment when your attention is worth money.

Upgrade bait before rods

Bait moves rarity e failure at the same time. Going from the Jujuba to the Legendary takes what you land from 65% to 96%.

Save the good ones for night

Legendary shows up 60% more at night, and that is when the Blood Moon can fall — it multiplies that by 5.
